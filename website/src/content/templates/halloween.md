## What it is

On most boards the columns are kinds of card, filled side by side. Here they are the steps of a retrospective, taken one after the other, each in a Halloween costume: setting the scene, gathering data, generating insights, deciding what to do and closing.

## Goal

A session with a beginning, a middle and an end on a single board. The team leaves knowing what happened, what it means and what it will do.

## When to use it

The sprint that ends around Halloween, with a team that likes to play. It also serves a new team that is learning what a retrospective is made of.

## When to pick another format

The titles name steps, not questions: they do not tell people what to write, so the facilitator has to bring a question for each column. For a team that wants to open the board and start writing, take [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/). At the end of the year, the seasonal board is the [Christmas Retro](../christmas/).

## How to run it

Prepare one question per column, and open the columns one at a time. The questions below are examples.

1. **Halloween treats!** Set the scene with something light: one card each, the sprint as a sweet or as a costume.
2. **Ghost stories!** Gather data. Ask what happened, and what haunted the sprint: the bug that came back, the task that would not end. No explanations yet.
3. **Trick or treat!** Generate insights. Read the stories and ask why. Which were tricks, and what caused them? Which were treats, and what made them possible?
4. **Pick a door!** Decide what to do. Each card is a door the team could open, a change to try.
5. **Spook of the sprint!** Close the retrospective with a last card each: the moment, the bug or the colleague that most deserves the title.

The actions come out of the fourth column: group the doors, vote, and write an action with an owner for each door the team chooses to open.
