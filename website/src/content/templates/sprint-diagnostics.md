## What it is

A guided sequence more than a sort. The four columns follow the order of a diagnosis: a rating of the sprint from 1 to 10, looked at through communication, collaboration, quality and pressure; then the strongest moment; then what could be done better; then the actions.

## Goal

A reading of the sprint in numbers that can be compared from one person to the next, each backed by a reason, and a list of changes with owners.

## When to use it

- When the team wants to check its health with the same questions sprint after sprint.
- When opinions about a sprint diverge and you want to see how far apart they are.
- With quiet members: a number and one sentence are easy to give.

## When to pick another format

A rating exposes its author. If people are not comfortable giving a low number, run a [Safety Check](../safety-check/) first, or use [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/), which asks for no score. If the sprint had one big problem that needs depth, take [Fishbone Analysis](../fishbone/).

## How to run it

1. **How was this sprint? Rate it 1–10 (communication, collaboration, quality, pressure)** Ask each person for a number plus one sentence on what drove it. Agree beforehand whether people give one overall number or one per angle.
2. **Big like or big dislike**: one card per person, the single strongest positive or negative moment of the sprint.
3. **What could we do even better or differently? How?** The "How?" matters: an improvement is phrased as a change, not as a complaint.
4. **Actions**: an owner and a date for each change. What people write here are proposals until the discussion confirms them.

Start with the ratings, and look at the spread before the average: a lone low number deserves a question, not a defence. Go through the strong moments next, then group and vote on the improvements, and close on the actions the team agreed on.
