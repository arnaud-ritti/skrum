## What it is

Three columns named after emotions. **Mad** collects what made people angry, the things that went wrong more than once. **Sad** collects disappointments: expectations that were never met. **Glad** collects what went well and is worth protecting.

## Goal

To put on the table what people carry and do not say in a status meeting, and to trace each feeling back to a cause the team can act on.

## When to use it

- After a hard sprint: a missed deadline, an incident, a conflict.
- When retrospectives have become polite and flat.
- When the members trust each other enough to speak about frustration.

## When to pick another format

The format needs trust. In a new team, or with a manager in the room, people may write little: a [Safety Check](../safety-check/) first tells you whether the room is ready, and [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/) covers the same ground without asking for emotions. After a calm sprint **Mad** stays empty and feels forced; [Happy, Meh, Sad](../happy-meh-sad/) is lighter.

## How to run it

1. **Mad**: ask what made people angry. The column is for what keeps going wrong, not for a single bad day. Remind everyone to aim at situations, not at colleagues.
2. **Sad**: ask what was disappointing. A useful prompt: what did you expect that did not happen?
3. **Glad**: ask what went well and is worth protecting next sprint. Fill it last, so that the writing does not end on complaints.

Group the cards and vote. Cards of **Mad** and **Sad** often describe the same event from two sides; say so when you reach them. For each topic, ask what caused the feeling before asking what to do about it. Not every card needs an action: sometimes having been heard is the result. Choose the causes the team can change, and give each action an owner and a date.
