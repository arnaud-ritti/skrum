## What it is

A tidying session applied to the way the team works. The picture is that of emptying a cupboard: each thing is taken in hand and the team decides whether it stays. Three columns receive the result: what brings joy and is happy to stay, what went wrong and should not stay, and what is worth improving and reusing.

## Goal

A lighter way of working: a few things kept on purpose, a few stopped, a few reshaped. The middle column is where the format earns its place, because it gives the team permission to stop doing something.

## When to use it

Take it when practices have piled up: meetings, reports, tools and rules whose reason nobody remembers. It fits the end of a project, a quarter or a year, and the weeks after two teams merge and find themselves with two of everything.

## When to pick another format

The board only sorts what already exists; nothing in it asks for something new. When the team needs new practices, [DAKI (Drop, Add, Keep, Improve)](../daki/) does the same sorting with a column to add. When the question is how solid things are rather than whether to keep them, take [Three Little Pigs](../three-little-pigs/).

## How to run it

Ask for one thing per card: a meeting, a habit, a tool, a rule.

1. **Brings you joy**: ask what went well and should stay, and why. A reason written down protects the thing the next time someone wants to cut it.
2. **Throw out**: ask what went wrong and should not stay. A card here is a proposal to stop, and it needs no replacement.
3. **Recycle**: ask what the team wants to improve and reuse. The idea is right and the form is not: the card says what would change.

Group the cards. The same thing in two columns is the first topic to discuss. Then vote on **Throw out** and **Recycle**. Stopping is an action like any other: write who announces it and from when.
