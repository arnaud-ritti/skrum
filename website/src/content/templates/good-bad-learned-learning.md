## What it is

Two columns review the sprint: what went well, what did not. Two treat learning as a personal goal: what did I want to learn and did I, what do I want to learn next. The fourth column of one retrospective feeds the third column of the following one.

## Goal

Learning becomes part of the work. Each person states a goal and what they need for it, the team sees it and can help, and the goal is looked at again a sprint later.

## When to use it

- In teams where growing skills is an explicit aim: junior members, a new technology, newcomers.
- Over several sprints in a row: the format pays off through repetition.
- When learning is always pushed back by delivery.

## When to pick another format

If the team has no interest in personal goals, the last two columns stay thin: [Original 4](../original-four/) collects lessons without asking for goals. When a sprint leaves urgent problems, give them the whole session with [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/).

## How to run it

1. **What went well?** Ask for successes worth repeating on purpose.
2. **What didn't go so well?** Ask what went wrong or cost the team time.
3. **What did I want to learn this sprint, and did I?** Ask each person for the learning goal they had set and how it went. The first time, nobody has a goal on record: let people write what they had hoped to learn.
4. **What do I want to learn next sprint?** Ask for a goal and for what the person needs to reach it.

Group, vote and discuss the second column, and agree on actions with an owner and a date. The two learning columns need no vote. For each goal of the last column, ask what would help: time set aside, a colleague to pair with, a piece of work to practise on. Those are actions too. Open the next retrospective by reading these goals again.
