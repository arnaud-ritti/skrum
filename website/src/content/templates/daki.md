## What it is

Four verbs, one per column: drop, add, keep, improve. The last one sets the format apart: it is the place for what is neither good nor bad, the practices that are nearly right and need fixing, not removing.

## Goal

An inventory of the team's ways of working, each with a verdict, and the decisions that follow.

## When to use it

- When processes have piled up and nobody has pruned them.
- After a team has worked together for a while and inherited habits it never chose.
- Around a reorganisation, to decide what to carry over.
- When the team is ready to remove things, not only to add them.

## When to pick another format

**Drop** is blunt. If the team is attached to its rituals, [KALM](../kalm/) reduces where DAKI removes. The format looks at practices, not at events or feelings: after a difficult sprint, start with [Mad, Sad, Glad](../mad-sad-glad/) or [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/).

## How to run it

1. **Drop**: ask what has no remaining value, such as a report nobody reads or a step already automated. A useful test: who would notice if it disappeared tomorrow?
2. **Add**: ask for a new practice to try. The column asks for one or two per sprint.
3. **Keep**: ask what works today and should be defended under pressure.
4. **Improve**: ask what is nearly right. Have people say what exactly is wrong with it.

Group and vote. **Drop** is the cheapest column to act on: if everyone agrees, the practice ends today, and the action is to tell whoever depends on it. **Improve** needs the most discussion: agree on what "fixed" looks like before naming an owner. From **Add**, take few. Read **Keep** last and check that no decision of the session threatens it.
