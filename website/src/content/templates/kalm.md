## What it is

Four columns named by the initials of the format: keep, add, more, less. There is no column for stopping. The hardest verdict a practice can receive is "less".

## Goal

Adjustments to the practices the team already has, a new practice to try, and an agreement on what is worth protecting.

## When to use it

- When things go reasonably well and need tuning, not repair.
- With a team that takes criticism of its habits personally: "less" is easier to hear than "stop".
- A few sprints after introducing new practices, to find their right dose.

## When to pick another format

When something has no value left, KALM has no place for it and people stretch **Less** to fit it in. Take [DAKI](../daki/) or [Starfish](../starfish/), which both let a practice go. KALM also looks at habits only: to review what happened in the sprint, use [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/).

## How to run it

1. **Keep**: ask what works today and would be the first thing lost under pressure.
2. **Add**: ask for something the team does not have yet and wants to try.
3. **More**: ask what already happens occasionally and should happen routinely.
4. **Less**: ask what is useful in smaller doses. The aim is to reduce it, not to abolish it.

Group the cards, vote, and discuss the most voted. Cards of **More** and **Less** describe quantities, so ask "how much?" until the answer can be checked: how often, how long, for whom. From **Add**, choose few: a new practice costs attention. Finish with **Keep**: read the cards and check that none of the actions you just agreed on undermines them. Each action gets an owner and a date.
