## What it is

Three questions about time, all addressed to "you": your plans, your choices, your day. It is a retrospective on one subject, not a review of the sprint.

## Goal

Each person leaves with ways to protect their time. The team finds out which leaks its members share: those are the ones that deserve a common rule.

## When to use it

- When people say they have no time, and days are cut up by meetings and interruptions.
- After a change in how the team works together: remote work, new meetings, a new on-call rota.
- As an occasional session on one theme, between regular retrospectives.

## When to pick another format

The format says nothing about the product or the sprint's results: for a regular retrospective, take [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/). If the tiredness goes deeper than the calendar, [Energy Levels](../energy-levels/) asks what drains people. To look at speed as a team matter, not a personal one, use [Speed Car](../speed-car/).

## How to run it

1. **Which of your plans or choices add time to your day?** Ask for the habits and choices that give hours back. Start here: these cards are tips the others can copy.
2. **Which personal choices steal time from your day?** Ask where time leaks: interruptions, meetings, rework. The question says "personal choices": have people look at their own part before blaming the calendar.
3. **How do or would you reclaim lost time?** Ask for concrete ways to win back the hours of the previous column. A good card answers one precise leak.

Group the leaks: those that several people share are a matter for the team. Vote on them, then look in the third column for a remedy to each of the most voted and turn it into a team agreement to try, with an owner and a date. Individual remedies stay with their authors.
