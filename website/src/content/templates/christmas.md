## What it is

A retrospective for the last days of the year. Three columns take the three tenses: Christmas past for what could have been different, Christmas present for what works great and for appreciation of colleagues, Christmas future for what people want to have. A fourth column asks for a favourite Christmas food and is there for the pleasure of it.

## Goal

To close the year together: look back without settling scores, thank each other, and leave with a few wishes that are ready to become commitments in January.

## When to use it

It is the last retro before the end-of-year break, and it can cover more than a sprint: a quarter, a project, the whole year. It suits a session where the mood counts as much as the output, and a remote team, for whom the food column gives a moment of ordinary conversation.

## When to pick another format

Do not assume that everyone on the team celebrates Christmas. When some do not, [Likes, Wishes, Wonders](../likes-wishes-wonders/) or the [Appreciation Retro](../appreciation/) give the same warm tone without the holiday. When the year ends on a serious problem, the festive dress rings false: take [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/). In the autumn there is [Halloween: Spooky Sprint](../halloween/).

## How to run it

1. **Christmas past**: ask what could have been different. The wording matters: different, not whose fault.
2. **Christmas present**: ask what works great today and who deserves thanks. A thank-you names a person and what they did.
3. **Christmas future**: ask what people want to have in the future, for the team, the product or their own work.
4. **Christmas foodie**: ask for a favourite Christmas food. It needs no vote and no discussion. It also works as a warm-up, filled before the three others.

Read the thanks aloud. Group and vote on the past and the future, then turn a few wishes into actions with an owner. Write them so that they still make sense after the break, when nobody remembers the discussion.
