## What it is

Two columns. **Plus** takes everything that is going well and should be repeated. **Delta** takes everything you want to change or that can be improved. Delta is the Greek letter used to note a change: the column is not a "minus", and its cards are worded as changes, not as complaints.

## Goal

Quick feedback, and a change or two to apply the next time.

## When to use it

- At the end of a meeting, a workshop, a training session or a day, for feedback on the session itself.
- When a few minutes are all you have.
- As a frequent, light check between full retrospectives.
- With a large group: two columns keep the board readable.

## When to pick another format

For a whole sprint, two columns are thin: there is no place for ideas, for what was learned or for how people feel. [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/) is the next step up. If the same cards come back to **Delta** every time, the subject needs more than a quick review: [Starfish](../starfish/) looks at the team's practices in detail, and [Fishbone Analysis](../fishbone/) looks for the causes of one problem.

## How to run it

1. **Plus**: ask what is going well and should be repeated. Specific cards help the most: which part of the session worked, and why.
2. **Delta**: ask what people want to change or what can be improved. Have them write the change itself: "start with the demo" says more than "the demo came too late".

Read both columns, group what repeats, and vote on **Delta**. Take the most voted cards and decide who changes what for the next time. Do not try to answer every card: a short format keeps its value only if what it decides is done. Begin the next session by checking that the changes were made.
