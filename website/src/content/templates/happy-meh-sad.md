## What it is

Three columns sorted by feeling instead of by topic. **Happy :)** takes the moments that gave the team energy, **Sad :(** what drained it or went clearly wrong, and **Meh :|** what was neither good nor bad. The middle column is what sets the format apart: indifference counts as information, not as nothing to report.

## Goal

A shared picture of how the sprint felt, and one or two changes that would move a Meh or a Sad card towards Happy next time.

## When to use it

It fits an ordinary sprint with no particular event to review, when you want an honest reading in little time. It suits a team new to retrospectives, because three faces need no explanation, and a team whose retrospectives have turned into lists of process complaints that no longer say how people are doing.

## When to pick another format

When there is real frustration in the team, Meh and Sad are too mild to carry it: [Mad, Sad, Glad](../mad-sad-glad/) gives anger its own column. When the worry is tiredness more than mood, take [Energy Levels](../energy-levels/). When the session must end with a written list of improvements, [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/) has a column for them.

## How to run it

1. **Happy :)** Ask what gave people energy this sprint: a moment, a colleague's help, something that finally worked.
2. **Meh :|** Ask what people felt nothing about: a meeting they sat through, a goal nobody cared about. Say that this column matters as much as the other two, because it is the one people skip.
3. **Sad :(** Ask what drained people or went clearly wrong. A card describes an event and its effect, not a colleague.

Group the cards that describe the same thing, then vote on what to discuss. Do not drop a large Meh group because it drew few votes: ask what would have to change for it to matter, or to disappear. Close each topic with an action item that has an owner.
