## What it is

Four columns, each a kind of weather, from the worst to the best. **Stormy** is for what blocked the team, **Rainy** for the difficulties it got past, **Cloudy** for the good experiences and the support between colleagues, and **Sunny** for goals met and work people feel good about. Despite the name, the four questions look back at the sprint, not ahead, and Cloudy sits on the pleasant side.

## Goal

The team tells apart what stopped it from what only slowed it down, sees what helped it through, and picks the storms it does not want to meet again.

## When to use it

For a sprint retrospective when the usual format has gone stale. Four levels give more nuance than good and bad: the format keeps a difficulty the team overcame apart from one that stopped it.

## When to pick another format

The difference between two neighbouring skies takes explaining, Cloudy most of all; with little time, the three columns of [Happy, Meh, Sad](../happy-meh-sad/) are quicker. To talk about the risks ahead and where the team is going, take [Sailboat](../sailboat/). When the question is how tired people are, [Energy Levels](../energy-levels/) asks it directly.

## How to run it

Read the four questions aloud first, so that nobody sorts cards by the name of the column alone.

1. **Stormy.** Ask what has been tempestuous: the issues faced, and what stopped the team from moving forward.
2. **Rainy.** Ask what has been troublesome: the challenges met, and how they were overcome.
3. **Cloudy.** Ask what has been rosy: the positive experiences, and the support received from colleagues.
4. **Sunny.** Ask what has been bright: whether the sprint goals were met, and whether people feel good about their contribution.

Group the cards and vote, giving priority to **Stormy**. For each top topic, look in **Rainy** for something that already worked on a smaller problem, and decide what to try. Record it as an action item with an owner. Before closing, read **Sunny** aloud, so that the session does not end on the storm.
