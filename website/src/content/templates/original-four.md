## What it is

Four questions. Two are about the work: what went well, what did not. Two are asked in the first person: what have I learned, what still puzzles me. There is no column for actions: they come out of the discussion.

## Goal

Besides the usual review, two things most formats lose: lessons worth passing on, and questions nobody has answered yet.

## When to use it

- At the end of a project, a release or a long piece of work.
- After working with a technology or a domain that was new to the team.
- When you suspect misunderstandings: the last column gives permission to say "I do not understand why we do this".

## When to pick another format

If the meeting has to end quickly on decisions, [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/) or [Start, Stop, Continue](../start-stop-continue/) get there faster. The questions are sober; when the team needs a change of scenery, a picture such as the [Sailboat](../sailboat/) covers similar ground.

## How to run it

1. **What went well?** Ask for successes worth naming out loud, however small.
2. **What didn't go so well?** Ask for problems and misses. Facts first: the reasons come in the discussion.
3. **What have I learned?** Ask for insights each person would pass to a teammate starting the same work.
4. **What still puzzles me?** Ask for open questions. Say that no question is too basic.

Begin the discussion with **What still puzzles me?** Some questions are answered on the spot by someone in the room, and that is a result in itself. Those that remain become actions: one person finds out and reports back. Then group and vote on **What didn't go so well?**, and agree on actions with an owner and a date for the most voted cards. Before closing, decide where the cards of **What have I learned?** are kept, so that they outlive the meeting.
