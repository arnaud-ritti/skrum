## What it is

Four columns, each with a symbol. Two look back at the sprint, thumbs up and thumbs down. One looks forward and collects ideas. The last one looks at people and gives credit.

## Goal

A balanced session: the problems are named, ideas get a place of their own and do not have to answer a problem, and colleagues are thanked by name.

## When to use it

- For a regular sprint, as an all-round format.
- With mixed groups or newcomers: the symbols explain the columns.
- When the team rarely takes the time to thank anyone.
- When ideas get lost because the retrospective only reviews the past.

## When to pick another format

Four columns spread the attention. In a short session, take the three of [Went well, To improve, Action ideas](../went-well-to-improve-actions/). If thanking each other is the whole purpose, the [Appreciation Retro](../appreciation/) gives it more room. If one problem needs to be taken apart, use [Fishbone Analysis](../fishbone/).

## How to run it

1. **👍 Thumbs up**: ask what went well this sprint.
2. **👎 Thumbs down**: ask what went wrong or felt frustrating. Both facts and feelings belong here.
3. **💡 New ideas**: ask for ideas worth trying, even half-formed ones. Say that an idea does not have to answer a card of the previous column.
4. **🏆 Recognition**: ask people to credit someone who made another person's work easier, with a name and what they did.

Start the discussion by reading **🏆 Recognition** aloud: it sets the tone and needs no vote. Then group and vote on the other three columns. Discuss the most voted cards of **👎 Thumbs down** with **💡 New ideas** beside them: an idea often answers a problem its author did not have in mind. Turn what the team agrees on into actions with an owner and a date.
