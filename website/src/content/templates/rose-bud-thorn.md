## What it is

A plant in three parts. The rose is in bloom: what is clearly working right now. The bud may bloom: early signs worth investing in before they fade. The thorn pricks: what hurts and keeps hurting until someone fixes it.

## Goal

Beside the usual good and bad, a list of young things, such as an idea, a practice tried once or a promising collaboration, that the team decides to look after before they disappear.

## When to use it

- When things are beginning: a new team, a new product, practices just introduced.
- When retrospectives only produce repairs and never an investment.
- For a short session: three columns, quick to explain.
- With newcomers or people from outside the team, who understand the picture at once.

## When to pick another format

After a hard sprint the gentle picture can feel beside the point: [Mad, Sad, Glad](../mad-sad-glad/) meets the mood better. If you need decisions about existing practices, [Start, Stop, Continue](../start-stop-continue/) asks for them directly. If the middle of your sprint is made of things that half worked, not of things that are starting, see [WWW](../www/).

## How to run it

1. **Rose**: ask what is clearly working right now.
2. **Bud**: ask for early signs: a first good result, a half-tested idea. A useful prompt: what would be worth more of our time before it fades?
3. **Thorn**: ask what hurts and will keep hurting until someone fixes it. One-off annoyances do not belong here.

Group the cards and vote on **Bud** and **Thorn**. A thorn calls for a fix, with an owner and a date. A bud calls for an action too, of another kind: some time set aside, a trial, a person who looks after it. Read **Rose** before closing, so that the team knows what it can rely on.
