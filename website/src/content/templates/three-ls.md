## What it is

Three columns: like, learned, lacked. In place of a column of problems, the format asks what was missing: information, time, access or a decision. The wording points at a gap to fill, not at a fault.

## Goal

The team knows what to keep, what the sprint taught it, and which missing things it has to obtain before the next one.

## When to use it

- For a regular sprint and a short session.
- With a team that is learning a lot: a new product, a new system, new members.
- When the problems column has turned into blame: "lacked" asks what was absent, not who failed.

## When to pick another format

The format has no place for what was present and harmful, such as a habit that gets in the way: [Start, Stop, Continue](../start-stop-continue/) has a column for that. For a period longer than a sprint, [Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for](../four-ls/) adds the wishes that go beyond the team.

## How to run it

1. **Like**: ask what each person appreciated about the sprint.
2. **Learned**: ask for new knowledge, about the product, the system or the team.
3. **Lacked**: ask what was missing. Have people name the thing and the moment it was needed: "the access to the staging logs on the day of the release" says more than "access".

Group the cards and vote, mostly on **Lacked**. For each of the most voted cards, ask who can provide what was missing, inside or outside the team. The action is to obtain it, with an owner and a date. Then read **Learned** together and pick what should be written down or shown to the rest of the team. **Like** needs no action: it tells you what not to break.
